<div class="measure">
  <p>HR technology conversations often start at the interface and stop there. The questions
  that determine whether a programme works sit one or two layers down: what the data means,
  where it comes from, who is allowed to change it, and what happens when two systems
  disagree.</p>
</div>

<div class="deflist">
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">HRIS architecture</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>Core HR and satellites, composability, what belongs in the
    system of record and what does not. Deciding this late is what makes integration
    expensive.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">Data &amp; integration</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>Data model, ontology, master data ownership, flows between
    systems and the reconciliation rules nobody wants to write down.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">AI &amp; automation</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>Where automation genuinely removes work, where it moves work
    somewhere less visible, and how to tell the difference before signing.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">Generative AI &amp; agents</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>What agentic patterns change in an HR process, what they
    require of the underlying data, and which controls have to exist first.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">People analytics</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>Metrics an executive committee can act on, built on
    definitions that survive scrutiny from finance.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">AI governance</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>Decision rights, transparency, human review, compliance and
    the boundary between decision support and decision making.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">Process design</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>Simplifying the process before automating it. Automating a bad
    process makes it permanent.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="deflist__row">
    <p class="deflist__term">Adoption</p>
    <div class="deflist__desc"><p>Capability, roles, incentives and measurement — the part that
    decides whether any of the above produces value.</p></div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <p class="callout__label">From code to boardroom</p>
  <p>I started close to the build: HR Access implementation and architecture across a group and
  around ten subsidiaries, leading a team of roughly five developers and twenty HR experts
  through modules and integrations. That was 2001 to 2006. I moved from there into talent, HR
  leadership, entrepreneurship and business leadership.</p>
  <p>I am not a full-time engineer today, and I would not claim to be. What that period gives
  me is different and harder to acquire later: the ability to sit in an executive committee and
  know, concretely, what the technical answer implies — and to sit with an architect and know
  what the business answer costs.</p>
</div>

<h2>What this changes in practice</h2>

<div class="measure">
  <ul>
    <li>Vendor demonstrations are assessed on the architecture behind them, not the interface
    in front of them.</li>
    <li>Data work is scheduled as a prerequisite, not as a workstream that runs in parallel and
    is compressed at the end.</li>
    <li>AI ambitions are sequenced against the state of the data model, so the pilot that
    succeeds can actually be scaled.</li>
    <li>Governance is designed with the architecture rather than added after the first
    incident.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
